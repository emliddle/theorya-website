Organizing Agency: The Theory A Manifesto

01Theory A

A philosophy of organizational design that begins with the assumptions underneath it.

Theory A begins with a simple observation: organizations are designed around assumptions, and those assumptions shape how work is organized, how decisions are made, and ultimately how value is created.

When the conditions beneath those assumptions change, organizational design must change with them.

Increasingly abundant intelligence is the most visible of those shifts today, and it is the reason the question feels so urgent. Theory A also reaches beyond AI, as a way of questioning inherited assumptions wherever they sit and redesigning organizations so that human and artificial agency create greater value together.

Explore why Theory A exists

Every organization reflects the assumptions it was designed around. Theory A begins by asking whether those assumptions still hold.

02The Signs

What it looks like when an organization's assumptions have stopped holding.

  • Productivity rises with AI while the organization itself grows no more capable than it was before.
  • People exercise the same judgment again and again, and none of it is captured as knowledge the organization can reuse.
  • Automation removes work without deciding which human capabilities need to remain.
  • People quietly compensate for the limits of AI tools, and the system has no way to learn from those interventions.
  • Decisions that improve today's economics quietly reduce tomorrow's options.

The organization is getting better at executing assumptions nobody has stopped to question.

Each of these is a Compounding problem, a sign that the system is dissipating capability faster than it builds it.

03The Harder Truth

You probably cannot get there from here.

Most organizations will try to become AI Native from inside the operating model they already have, and very few of them will succeed. The structures that made the old model work are the same structures that keep the new one from forming: the approval chains, the fixed roles, and the governance built for a human pace.

For many corporate functions, the more honest answer is to build the new operating structure alongside the existing one and migrate into it, function by function, as it proves itself.

It is a harder conversation to have than presenting a roadmap, and it is the one that gives a new operating model room to form.

04The Principles

Five principles that work together as one connected system of organizational design.

Each principle is an enduring question, and together they sit at the center of Theory A.

The five principles are meant to be read together, because each one shapes the conditions for the others.

Abundance changes the conditions of what is possible. Outcome establishes what the organization is trying to accomplish. Orchestration determines how agency works together. Form determines the configuration that enables it. And Compounding asks what operating that design causes the organization to become over time.

The system keeps moving, because whatever compounds changes the capabilities, assumptions, and possibilities available to the organization, and those become the new conditions for what comes next.

The Principles of Theory A

Abundance

What economic reality are you designing for?

When previously scarce capabilities become abundant, economic value shifts, and every mode of production changes which constraints matter. Organizations designed around yesterday's scarcity will optimize the wrong things. The first design question is therefore about constraint: what has become the real limit on value now that intelligence is abundant?

Orchestration

How are different forms of agency designed to work together?

The central challenge has moved past getting people to adopt technology. Organizations now need to deliberately orchestrate human and artificial agency: deciding where each acts independently, where they collaborate, where judgment must remain human, and how action stays aligned to a shared purpose. The value comes from how the two forms of agency are arranged together, which is why treating AI as a substitute for people misses most of it.

Outcome

What is the organization fundamentally organizing around?

Agency creates value when it is directed toward meaningful outcomes. Organizations designed around roles, functions, and inherited boundaries can fragment action. Theory A organizes capability around outcomes and gives people and systems the context and authority to act toward them.

Form

What structures create or constrain agency?

Every structure shapes agency, whether or not anyone designed it to. Decision rights, information flows, incentives, technology, governance, and boundaries determine where agency can emerge and whether distributed action becomes coordinated capability, and their architecture either serves that purpose or quietly works against it.

Compounding

Does the system increase or dissipate organizational capability over time?

Over time, every organization either compounds agency or dissipates it, and each structural decision pushes in one direction or the other. A Theory A organization learns through action: experience strengthens judgment, knowledge becomes reusable, digital capability improves, and each cycle leaves the organization more capable than the one before.
